The organizations getting the most value from their data people are the ones who've figured out what data analysts actually do.

It sounds obvious when you say it out loud, but there's a real difference between having someone in a data role and having someone who can actually do data analysis work. That difference matters tremendously for how well your digital workplace actually serves people.

A true business data analyst doesn't just pull reports or move numbers around. They're asking the questions that help you understand what's really happening in your environment. They're the ones who notice patterns that nobody else sees yet. They're connecting dots between what people are asking for and what the actual data says about their experience. They're building the bridge between what your organization thinks is happening and what's genuinely happening.

When you give someone like this the room to work, the space to dig into problems, and the trust to follow the data where it leads, something shifts. You start getting insights that change how you make decisions. You stop guessing about what your employees need and start knowing. Your service teams get better at their work because they're working with clarity instead of assumption.

The challenge isn't finding people who can do this work. The challenge is creating an environment where they can actually do it. That means giving them time to explore data without it being treated as a distraction from 'real work.' It means letting them ask questions even when those questions might complicate a tidy narrative. It means building a culture where data-driven thinking is genuinely valued, not just paid lip service.

When this works well, your employees feel it. They notice that decisions are being made with actual insight into their experience. They see that the organization is paying attention to what's actually happening, not just what looks good in a meeting. That builds trust. That improves how people engage with the services you're providing.

The organizations that are thriving in their digital workplace functions tend to have one thing in common: they've got someone in a data role who's genuinely enabled to do analysis work, and that person's insights are shaping decisions across the organization. It's not complicated. It's not about fancy tools or big budgets. It's about recognizing that this work has real value and creating the conditions for it to happen.

Your customers, internal and external, can tell when an organization truly understands what's going on versus when they're making decisions in the dark. The difference is often someone who gets to do real analytical work. That's worth thinking about as you build out your team and your culture.
