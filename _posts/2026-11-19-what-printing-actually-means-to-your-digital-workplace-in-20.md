Enterprise printing has quietly become something worth thinking carefully about, not because it's broken, but because it's finally working in ways that actually matter to how people experience their workplace.

There's this interesting moment happening right now where organizations are realizing that printing and peripherals aren't just infrastructure problems to solve and forget about. They're part of the actual experience people have when they sit down at their desk or walk into a collaboration space. How smoothly a printer works, how obvious the process is, whether a device just does what someone needs it to do without friction — these things shape whether someone feels supported or frustrated.

What's genuinely encouraging is how this is shifting the conversation. Teams aren't asking 'how do we reduce printing?' the way they might have framed it five years ago. Instead they're asking 'what does good printing experience look like for our actual people, in our actual spaces, doing their actual work?' That's a healthier question. It acknowledges that printing isn't going away, and maybe that's fine. Maybe the goal is just to make it effortless.

The enterprises thinking most clearly about this are treating peripherals the way they treat any other part of the digital workplace. They're mapping out what their different user groups actually need. They're thinking about the relationship between mobile work and printing. They're considering how to make device management feel invisible rather than burdensome. Some are building really thoughtful partnerships with their vendors and service providers, creating relationships where both sides understand the actual business outcomes that matter.

There's also something nice happening around consolidation and simplification. Organizations are moving toward environments where the number of different device types and drivers and configuration scenarios is genuinely manageable. That might sound boring, but it's actually liberating. When you're not dealing with sprawl, your team can focus on the experience rather than firefighting.

The peripheral ecosystem is also genuinely evolving in useful directions. Devices are getting more secure by default. Analytics are becoming more helpful without being creepy. Integration with broader workplace management tools is happening in ways that make sense operationally. And importantly, vendors are building things that acknowledge how work has actually changed, not how they think it should change.

If you're leading digital workplace or service management right now, thinking about printing and peripherals in 2026 isn't about catching up to some revolution. It's about recognizing that the foundation of a good digital workplace includes making sure that the physical interaction people have with their devices feels thoughtful and supported. That's worth getting right, and it turns out there's a lot of good thinking happening on this topic.

It's one of those areas where being intentional, rather than just reactive, makes a real difference in how people experience their work.
