Forrester's latest research tells us that AI will consume about a fifth of corporate technology spending by 2027. That's a substantial slice of the budget pie, and it's worth sitting with for a moment because it reveals something genuine about where organizations see their future heading.

Here's what strikes me about this figure: it's not driven by panic or hype, but by a pretty straightforward realization that AI genuinely changes how work gets done. When you're responsible for digital services, for employee experience, for keeping systems running smoothly, you start seeing the places where AI actually helps. The repetitive query that your service desk handles a hundred times a day. The routine ticket routing that someone spent hours configuring. The pattern recognition that takes a human analyst three hours and an AI system three seconds.

The budget shift itself is interesting because it doesn't happen in a vacuum. Organizations don't just sprinkle AI on top of everything they're already doing. They're actively rethinking how they deliver services, how they structure their teams, and what kinds of work they want their people focusing on. When you start allocating real resources toward AI capabilities, you're making a statement about priorities. You're saying that automation, efficiency, and thoughtful technology deserve genuine investment.

It's also worth noting that not all of that budget goes to flashy new capabilities. A meaningful chunk goes toward integrating AI into the systems already running your digital environment. It's the careful work of connecting things together, making sure AI tools work well with your existing platforms, and building the governance around these tools so they actually serve your organization's needs rather than creating new problems.

What's particularly human about all this is that organizations are learning as they go. There's no blueprint that says 'if you're company size X, allocate Y percent to AI and Z percent to everything else.' Teams are making thoughtful choices about where AI actually adds value versus where it creates unnecessary complexity. That's the work of digital leaders who understand their environment deeply enough to know what will actually make things better.

The fact that more than half of IT executives expect to miss their targets alongside this shift tells us something too. Change of this magnitude requires real rethinking, careful planning, and honest conversations about what success actually looks like. That's harder than just spending the money, and it's exactly the kind of work that builds organizational capability over time.

This budget allocation feels less like a trend passing through and more like a genuine reorientation of how companies approach technology. And that's worth paying attention to, regardless of where you sit in your organization.
