When vendor leaders take time to actually know their clients, something shifts in how the work gets done.

There's a particular kind of confidence that emerges when you're working with a vendor whose executives genuinely know you. Not just your name and your department, but the shape of your challenges, the constraints you're working within, the goals that matter to your organization. That knowledge doesn't come through a sales deck or a support ticket. It comes from presence.

Sales teams and support staff do tremendously important work. They're often the first to hear what's not working, what customers need, where the friction lives. They're skilled at their roles and they care about getting things right. But there's something that only happens when someone senior enough to make decisions takes the time to sit across from you and listen. It's not about hierarchy or formality. It's about access to judgment.

When an executive knows your environment, they can anticipate problems before you articulate them. They can suggest approaches that actually fit into how you work, not just how their product is designed to work. They understand the difference between what you're asking for and what you actually need. They can say yes and no with real weight behind it because they're not operating from a script.

Think about how that changes a partnership. Your sales rep brings a great idea from another customer, but your executive contact already understands why that might not apply to you. Your support team escalates an issue, and there's someone in the room who knows this isn't just a technical problem, it's part of a larger initiative that matters. When budget season comes around, you're not just a line item being defended by a sales team. There's someone who advocates for you because they actually know what you're trying to accomplish.

This matters most when things get complicated. When a deployment doesn't go as planned, or your needs shift, or you're trying to figure out if a new feature actually solves something or just adds complexity. That's when having a real relationship with people who can think and decide makes all the difference.

It's worth asking yourself what your vendor relationships look like right now. Who do you actually know? Who knows you? If the only conversations happening are through the sales channel or the support queue, something valuable is being left on the table. The best partnerships are built by people at every level, including the ones who can shape strategy and allocate resources.

Your digital workplace deserves vendors who show up for it. Not just at the beginning, but throughout. That's how good work gets done.
