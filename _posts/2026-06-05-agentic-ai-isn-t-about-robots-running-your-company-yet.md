## Stop Calling Everything AI

There's a reason your inbox is full of vendor pitches about "agentic AI." It sounds impressive. It sounds like the future. It also sounds like the same bullshit we heard about cloud computing, big data, and blockchain before everyone figured out what those things actually meant.

Let's start with clarity. Most AI tools you're using right now are passive. They wait for you to ask them a question, give them a prompt, or tell them what to do. ChatGPT is passive. Copilot is passive. They suggest, they complete, they summarize. You remain the decision maker and the action taker.

Agentic AI is different because it actually does things without you explicitly asking it to do each step. An agentic system can plan a sequence of actions, execute them, check the results, and adapt if something goes wrong. It doesn't need a human to greenlight each individual step along the way.

## What This Means for Your Digital Workplace

Here's where it gets relevant to your job. Your digital workplace is drowning in repetitive tasks that require actual decision making but not much creativity. Request workflows get stuck because someone didn't fill out a field. Expense reports need three approvals but the first approver is on vacation. Employee onboarding has seventeen manual handoffs between HR, IT, Finance, and the hiring manager. These aren't problems that Copilot can solve by being smarter at suggestions.

Agentic AI could actually handle these. An agentic system could monitor your ticketing queue, identify patterns in requests, gather missing information automatically, route approvals to backup owners when people are out, and escalate legitimately complex issues to humans. It could coordinate across multiple systems without someone writing a five-step workflow document that nobody reads.

The catch is that your digital workplace has to be set up for this to work. And most workplaces are not.

## The Gap Between Hype and Reality

Agentic AI requires a few things that are surprisingly uncommon. First, your systems have to talk to each other properly. If your HR system doesn't integrate with your ticketing system, and your ticketing system doesn't speak to your finance platform, then no agent can actually do anything useful across those boundaries. It can only make recommendations, which brings us back to passive AI.

Second, you need clear process documentation. An agentic system needs to understand what success looks like and what the rules are. If your onboarding process exists only in someone's head or in seventeen different Confluence pages that contradict each other, an agent can't work with it. You have to actually know your own process first.

Third, you need appropriate guardrails. This is the part vendors downplay because it's less exciting than "autonomous agents." An agent that can approve expense reports is useful until it approves a fraudulent one. An agent that can provision user access saves time until it gives someone access they shouldn't have. You need audit trails, exception handling, and clear limits on what an agent can decide versus what needs human judgment.

Most organizations skip these prerequisites and jump straight to "we need agentic AI" because they heard about it on a podcast.

## Where to Actually Start

You don't need to wait for the perfect agentic platform. Start by identifying one actual process that causes you regular pain. Not the one you think is broken. The one your IT team and your end users both complain about repeatedly. Is it password resets? Is it provisioning? Is it the approval workflow for software requests?

Make that process transparent. Document it step by step. Get the people who actually do it involved. Map out where decisions happen and where it's just mechanical work. That's where agentic capability would actually help.

Then look at what you'd need to automate it properly. Usually you'll discover it's less about needing fancy new AI and more about finally integrating systems you should have integrated years ago.

The vendors will keep telling you that agentic AI is a revolution that requires a complete platform replacement. Some of them are right eventually. But most of your digital workplace problems don't require a revolution. They require competent integration and honest process discipline. The AI part, when it actually shows up, will just be the useful finishing touch.

## The Verdict

Agentic AI is real and will matter. Just not in the way the marketing materials suggest. It won't magically fix broken processes. It won't replace human judgment where it counts. What it might do, eventually, is handle the truly mechanical coordination work that your organization currently wastes human attention on. Get your house in order first, and you'll know whether you actually need it.
