When your organization spans continents, staffing a service desk isn't about coverage—it's about building something that feels local everywhere.

Large distributed organizations face a genuinely interesting puzzle when it comes to service desk models. It's not a problem to solve so much as a design challenge to think through carefully. The question isn't whether you need people around the clock—of course you do. The question is how you build a service desk that feels coherent, connected, and genuinely helpful to people working across wildly different time zones and business contexts.

Some organizations lean toward a follow-the-sun model, where coverage literally passes like a relay across geographies. There's something elegant about this approach. It means someone in Tokyo, London, and São Paulo can each have a service desk that feels local—staffed by people who understand their timezone, their working hours, their business rhythms. The handoff can feel natural when teams trust each other and share clear documentation and context.

Others build regional hubs where expertise clusters geographically. You might have a stronger presence in EMEA, APAC, and the Americas, with teams that know their regions deeply. This often means people can develop genuine relationships with the business units they serve. A team in Singapore isn't just handling tickets; they understand how insurance works in that market, what the regulatory environment looks like, what keeps the local finance team up at night.

Then there's the hybrid space, where you might combine elements. Perhaps you have a lean follow-the-sun layer for true 24-hour coverage, with stronger regional teams handling deeper work during business hours. Or you distribute specialized expertise across zones, so people have access to the exact skills they need regardless of when they're working.

What makes this genuinely interesting is that none of these approaches is inherently right. What matters is alignment with how your organization actually works. Does your business operate continuously, or does most of the real work happen in concentrated time windows? Are your teams deeply distributed or do they cluster geographically? How much does local context matter versus global standardization? How do your people actually want to work?

The warmth comes from getting this right. When a service desk feels like it understands you—your timezone, your context, your rhythm—everything else works better. People feel taken care of. Issues get resolved faster not because the process is perfect, but because there's genuine connection between the service desk and the people they're serving.

Building that across continents takes thought and intention. But that's exactly what makes it worth thinking about carefully.
